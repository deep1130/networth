1. IRS Federal Retirement & HSA Limits Current IRS Limits

Official IRS annual limits for retirement vehicles and pre-tax shields:

  • 401(k) / 403(b) / TSP: $24,500 elective limit in 2026. Catch-up (age 50+) is $8,000 (higher SECURE 2.0 catch-up of $11,250 for ages 60–63).
  • IRA (Traditional & Roth): $7,500 annual contribution limit. Single phase-out begins at $150k (use Backdoor Roth IRA above limit).
  • Health Savings Account (HSA): $4,550 single / $9,100 family in 2026. Federally triple tax-advantaged (tax-free contribution, growth, and medical withdrawal).
  • Standard Deduction: $16,100 Single / $32,200 MFJ in 2026.
2. Federal 0% Capital Gains Bracket $0 Federal Tax

The single biggest tax superpower for early retirees living on investments:

  • 0% Federal Bracket: Long-term capital gains and qualified dividends are taxed at 0% up to $49,450 of taxable income for single filers ($98,900 MFJ).
  • Stacked with Standard Deduction: Combined with the $16,100 standard deduction, you can realize up to $65,550/year in pure capital gains & dividends and owe $0.00 IN FEDERAL TAX!
  • Tax-Gain Harvesting: If income is below this threshold, you can sell and instantly repurchase appreciated funds to permanently step up cost-basis tax-free (no wash-sale rule on gains).
3. Penalty-Free Early Access (Pre-59½) Roth Ladder & Rule 55

How to access retirement savings decades before traditional retirement age without penalties:

  • 5-Year Roth Conversion Ladder: Convert traditional 401(k)/IRA balances to Roth during low-income early retirement years (taxed at 0%–12%). Withdraw converted principal 100% tax-free and penalty-free after 5 tax years.
  • Rule of 55: If you separate from service in or after the calendar year you turn 55, you can take penalty-free distributions directly from your current 401(k) plan.
  • 72(t) SEPP: Substantially Equal Periodic Payments allow penalty-free distributions from traditional IRAs at any age based on IRS actuarial tables.
4. $0 Silver vs. Subsidized Gold Strategy 100% Covered Option

Understanding how federal & NJ subsidies eliminate healthcare costs in early retirement:

  • The 100% Subsidized $0 Silver Plan (≤150% FPL): Yes! If your reportable MAGI is kept under 150% FPL (~$23,500/yr for single), the benchmark contribution is legally 0.0% of income. Federal APTC + NJHPS state subsidies cover 100% of the premium ($0.00 / month), AND you receive the maximum 94% CSR upgrade ($0 deductible, $0–$5 copays)!
  • The Gold Plan Route ($320/mo): If you want to realize more investment income ($35k–$50k MAGI) or do larger Roth conversions, Gold plans provide 80% coverage and $0–$1,000 deductibles with fixed $15–$30 copays for ~$320/mo ($3,840/yr) without strict CSR income ceilings.
  • Cash Subsidies Apply to Any Metal: Federal Premium Tax Credits and NJHPS are cash vouchers. You can apply your credit to a $0 Silver plan or upgrade to a Gold plan.
5. The "Spend $100k, Report $35k" Playbook Portfolio Arbitrage

In early retirement, spending does NOT equal taxable income (MAGI):

  • Zero-MAGI Outflows: Cash reserves, return of brokerage cost-basis, and Roth IRA contribution withdrawals generate $0 reportable MAGI.
  • Living Large on $35k MAGI: Drawing $40k capital basis + $25k Roth principal + $35k dividends/gains lets you spend $100,000/yr in cash flow while locking in subsidized Gold healthcare ($320/mo) and $0 federal income tax!
6. New Jersey State Tax Codes & Mandate NJ-1040 Rules

State-specific rules to navigate in New Jersey:

  • NJ Health Mandate: NJ penalizes uninsured residents on Line 52 of NJ-1040 (the greater of $695/adult or 2.5% of income). Subsidized GetCoveredNJ coverage avoids this penalty.
  • HSA Taxation: NJ does NOT recognize HSAs. Contributions are subject to NJ state tax, and annual HSA interest/dividends are reportable on NJ-1040.
  • ANCHOR Rebate: Direct cash rebate of $450/yr (renters) or $1,000–$1,500/yr (homeowners) for incomes under $150k/$250k.
  • Age 62+ Pension Exclusion: At age 62, NJ allows excluding up to $100,000/yr of pension/IRA withdrawals completely tax-free if total income ≤ $100k.
